# Data scope and evidence

The assessment asks for corporate and M&A partners across every office of fifteen firms. This delivery intentionally exposes collection gaps: there is no independently checked denominator and no firm is marked complete.

`data/sources.json` enumerates the exact fifteen firms. `data/discovery.json` contains public-search discovery candidates; some are practice pages or unsuitable profiles. A URL in that file is not a verified record. Only `data/seed.json` records accepted official-profile observations, and only their actual source retrieval timestamps are used. `data/history.json` stores observed differences and actual collector runs. `data/coverage.json` is a generated report rather than a second database.

The dataset is a sample skewed toward public M&A and senior figures surfaced by search. A collector returning 7/7 candidates means seven known candidates passed; it does not mean the firm has seven relevant partners. English labels, finite office aliases, normalised practice categories and bounded requests can omit relevant people. Office membership comes from profile contact information, not the location of a deal or an educational institution.

Quinn Emanuel's explicit M&A litigation profiles are included as **M&A Litigation (adjacent)**. General corporate disputes are excluded. Corporate Tax is excluded as an interpretation of Corporate, and Milbank's structured Person practice labels are used to exclude tax/transport-only profiles. Other corporate finance and industry practices may be broader than a recruiter's target desk: inspect the original biography and narrow by practice before contacting anyone.

The first development extraction found false positives and office errors. Those were investigated against cached real HTML, pinned in synthetic regression fixtures, and excluded/corrected before the release baseline was rebuilt. Development scratch files are ignored and are not part of the portable seed. Parser corrections are not presented as real-world market events. Excerpts and hashes come from actual responses; no partner names or professional facts were fabricated. Synthetic Jane Example fixtures occur only in automated tests.

Successful responses are stored locally as `data/raw/<sha256>.html`. The SHA-256 covers the decoded HTML encoded as UTF-8. Snapshots are saved as bytes so Windows newline conversion cannot invalidate them. Raw pages are excluded from Git to avoid redistributing full biographies and scripts. The release repository retains short evidence excerpts, source links and timestamps; rerunning a permitted refresh creates local snapshots. Office fields retained from an older observation carry an older field-level hash and timestamp.

The collector implements robots policies and conservative access handling. A missing robots file is different from permission to disregard site terms. No login, CAPTCHA bypass, paywall access, proxy rotation, private enrichment or personal contact inference is used. Do not take this engineering policy as a legal determination. Before routine commercial use, review site terms, purpose, retention, rights requests and access permissions with the appropriate person at the business. The local prototype does not require those policy decisions to be invented on behalf of Pinpoint.

Public business contacts can occur incidentally inside excerpts. No private contact information or sensitive personal attributes are collected. Full HTML snapshots remain local. Before making even public professional data broadly available, review the repository visibility and data distribution choices yourself.

A failing request does not overwrite a person with an empty record. Missing pages produce review-only signals; scope/title extraction failures retain the last-known profile and appear in run messages. An office missing from an otherwise accepted same-version profile remains explicitly last known. This conservative behaviour trades recall and immediacy for avoiding false departures and false removals.
